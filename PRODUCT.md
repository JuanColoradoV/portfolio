# PRODUCT.md

**register:** brand

## What this is

The personal portfolio of Juan David Colorado Vargas, a Senior Product Designer & Lead in Medellín, Colombia. It is the primary artifact in an active job search for a remote Lead/Senior product design role at an enterprise consultancy or B2B product company.

The design is the product. A visitor's impression is the thing being made.

## Who reads it

Design directors and technical recruiters at enterprise consultancies (EPAM, Slalom, Wizeline, Globant) and B2B/AI product companies. They open it alongside a CV and a LinkedIn profile, usually for under two minutes, usually to decide whether to schedule a call.

They are not looking for beautiful screens. They are looking for evidence of judgment: did this person decide anything, and can they defend it.

## The core idea: every number opens

In July 2026 an audit of this site found that three of its four headline metrics were mislabeled and one chart was fabricated outright. All of it was corrected. What survived is a set of small, honest, fully sourced results.

That episode is the brand. Juan's own line, already on the site: *"I'd rather ship a smaller design I can defend with a Maze result than a bigger one I can only defend with taste."*

So the site makes defensibility literal. Every quantitative claim carries its receipt in the open: the method, the sample size, and the caveat that weakens it. Not in a footnote. As the visual system.

This is not a humility gesture. It is the strongest available differentiator, because almost no portfolio does it, and because a designer who publishes the caveat is signalling the exact seniority these readers are screening for.

## Voice

Exacting, annotated, unflinching. The register of a lab notebook or an audit trail, not of a marketing page.

- State the result, then immediately state what it does not prove.
- Never inflate. "9 of 10" over "90%", because the sample stays visible.
- The failure is more interesting than the success. The tenth participant who could not find the prompt taught more than the nine who could.

## Anti-references

- **Dribbble-shot portfolios.** Floating device mockups on gradient backgrounds, zero reasoning, invented metrics.
- **The SaaS hero-metric strip.** Big number, small label, three across. Explicitly rejected for this site.
- **Case studies that are only screens.** Pretty output with no decision trail.
- **Anything that implies a study that did not happen.** This site fabricated a chart once. Never again: no generated product UI presented as real work, no projected figures, no invented cohorts.

## Strategic principles

1. Evidence is the aesthetic. If a claim cannot open, it should not be a claim.
2. Small and sourced beats big and unverifiable.
3. Show the caveat. It reads as seniority, not weakness.
4. Real artifacts only. Diagrams of actual process are imagery; fabricated screenshots are fraud.
5. The work goes above the fold. Nobody scrolls a stranger's portfolio on faith.
